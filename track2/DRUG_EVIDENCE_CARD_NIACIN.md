- **Molecular target and direction of action; primary pharmacology source:** NAD+ precursor. High-dose nicotinic acid raised blood NAD+ 4–6-fold in healthy individuals (K5-E19). Any BubR1 effect would act through SIRT2-dependent K668 deacetylation (K5-E18).

It is irrelevant if the disease gene is TRIP13, CEP57 or CENATAC, if the BUB1B variants are in cis, if the missense change is benign, or if both alleles are null.
- **Exact rescue hypothesis and competing explanations:** nicotinic acid raises NAD+, so BUBR1 is deacetylated and stabilised, giving fewer segregation errors. Competing explanations: NAD+ rises without any change in BUBR1; pleiotropic sirtuin effects.
- **Target expression, relevant compartment, exposure evidence and model concentration range:** blood NAD+ data only. Exposure in progenitor cells is unknown. Cells need NAPRT to use nicotinic acid.
- **Disease-model evidence and transfer limits:** in mice, NMN and SIRT2 overexpression raise BubR1, and SIRT2 extends the lifespan of BubR1(H/H) mice (genetic evidence and a related compound). Niacin itself has not been tested on BubR1.
